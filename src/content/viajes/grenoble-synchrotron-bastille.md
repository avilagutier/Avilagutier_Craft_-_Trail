From **February 27 to March 6, 2022**, I traveled to Grenoble in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of southeastern France for a hybrid expedition: advanced scientific research and solo city exploration surrounded by alpine peaks.

### 🔬 Synchrotron Research at ESRF
The primary purpose of the stay was an intensive experimental campaign at the **European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF)**. 
- Conducted operando measurements and beamtime data collection.
- Executed sample characterization using **X-ray Absorption Near Edge Structure (XANES)** and **X-ray Excited Optical Luminescence (XEOL)** to study advanced functional materials and understand electronic structure and local coordination under radiation.

### 🏔️ La Bastille & Alpine Panorama
Taking a break from the experimental hall, I headed up to **Fort de La Bastille**, an 19th-century military fortress perched on the foothills of the Chartreuse mountain range.
- The climb (and iconic spherical cable car, "Les Bulles") rewards you with an astonishing panoramic view over the entire valley of Grenoble, framed by the snow-capped Belledonne and Vercors massifs.
- One of the highlights of the visit was watching the light shift over the Isère river and the alpine summits as the afternoon progressed.

### 🏛️ City Center & Night Walks
Down in the historic center (*Vieille Ville*), Grenoble reveals its rich university and cultural heritage with vibrant squares, cobblestone pathways, and traditional French brasseries.
- Walking around during the day was lively and architecturally captivating.
- At night, however, some of the quiet alleys took on a noticeably moody, shadowed, and eerie atmosphere once the shops and bustling afternoon life wound down.
